# Public Safety Dashboard

An Excel dashboard for analyzing police effectiveness and road safety over the period 07.12.2024 – 08.12.2025. The project summarizes interventions, arrests, drunk drivers, accidents, fatalities, and injuries, with a focus on weekly patterns and operational efficiency.

> **Note:** The dashboard workbook is in Polish.

## Project overview

The dashboard presents a year-long analysis of public safety and road traffic risk. It combines KPI summaries, daily and monthly trends, descriptive statistics, and operational insights about how police workload relates to prevention effectiveness.

## Repository structure

```text
public_safety_dashboard/
└── public_safety_dashboard.xlsx
```

- `public_safety_dashboard.xlsx` — the Excel workbook containing the dashboard.

## Dashboard sections

### KPI summary

Shows annual totals and key measures such as:

- total interventions,
- arrests made on the spot,
- wanted-person arrests,
- drunk drivers,
- road accidents,
- fatalities,
- injuries.

### Weekly patterns

Highlights how risk changes by day of week and whether the day is a weekend or a working day.

### Monthly trends

Compares interventions, arrests, drunk driving incidents, accidents, fatalities, and injuries across months.

### Descriptive statistics

Provides average, median, minimum, maximum, total, and standard deviation for the main indicators.

### Operational insights

Includes observations about police workload, prevention efficiency, and correlations between intervention volume and arrest effectiveness.

## Key insights

The dashboard highlights several findings:

- Friday shows the highest average number of accidents.
- Weekend days have a higher average number of drunk drivers than working days.
- The relationship between police workload and effectiveness appears negative in high-load periods.
- Days without fatalities are rare.
- The analysis suggests that prevention efforts should be adjusted to traffic peaks and weekend alcohol risk.
